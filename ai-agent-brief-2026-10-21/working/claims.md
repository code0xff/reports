# Claims

- c01: Mastercard opened early access to Agent Pay for Machines — buyer- and seller-side APIs, a developer toolkit, and a sandbox — moving the product from a June announcement to a pilot developers can test, following end-to-end pilots with Cloudflare, Ant International, BVNK, AIsa, Base, and Coinbase.
- c02: At Cloudflare Connect, an AI agent made nine x402-settled payments across two providers (AIsa and Cloudflare AI Gateway) inside an approved budget, demonstrating Mastercard's stablecoin/fiat settlement path end to end rather than as a slide.
- c03: Visa, BILL, and Billtrust launched a live pilot — not a demo — in which an AI agent registered under Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol and Agentic Directory authenticates itself to a supplier portal and completes buyer-approved invoice payments without shared logins or CAPTCHA-style checks.
- c04: In the Visa/BILL/Billtrust pilot, the agent has no authority to decide whether to pay; it only executes invoice payments the buyer already approved, which keeps the authorization boundary at the human mandate rather than at the agent.
- c05: Circle and Proof demonstrated an AI agent paying a merchant in USDC from a Circle Agent Wallet after presenting a Proof-issued identity credential (via the x401 protocol) proving a human had approved that specific purchase — a Money20/20 demo, not a production rollout.
- c06: Figma's co-founder Dylan Field publicly criticized MCP as "not a perfect protocol" that favors frontier AI labs, while Figma simultaneously announced 20 new MCP integration partners at its first Config conference, including a reversal on supporting Pi.
- c07: Security researchers disclosed, through public GitHub issues (one tracing to a HackerOne report Coinbase had already received), that x402's own reference paywall implementations in TypeScript/Express, Java, and the legacy v1 middleware can be bypassed entirely via path-normalization mismatches (`%2F`, `%2e%2e`) or exploited for reflected XSS — and as of this brief, the fix PRs remain open and unmerged.
- c08: None of the three Money20/20 announcements change who decides an agent may spend; all three keep a signed human mandate or buyer approval as the authorization root, with the agent only executing within it.
