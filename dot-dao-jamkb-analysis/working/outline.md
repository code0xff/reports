# Outline — DOT DAO and the Need for $JAMKB: Analysis and Rebuttal

Primary language: English. Alternate: Korean.

## 1. Abstract
Compressed statement of what the proposal is, which parts of its reasoning
survive scrutiny, and which do not. Written last.

## 2. Introduction
- What Gavin Wood proposed on 2026-06-22 and why a new token for Polkadot
  matters beyond tokenomics trivia.
- Framing: this is a *personal opinion* post, not an enacted roadmap item.
- Scope of this report: reconstruct the argument as a chain of premises,
  test each link, then state the strongest available rebuttal.

## 3. Background — footprint as an inelastic resource
- JAM's two storage tiers: the Data Lake (availability) vs. *footprint*
  (state held in every validator's RAM).
- Why immediacy, not throughput, is the binding constraint.
- What the JAM Gray Paper already specifies about service balances and
  storage thresholds — i.e. the mechanism $JAMKB would denominate.
- JAM reference hardware and where the ~20 GB figure comes from.

## 4. The proposal, anatomized
- The argument as an explicit premise chain (P1…P5 → C).
- $JAMKB's stated parameters: ~21 M fixed supply, 1 token = 1 KB,
  perpetual hold, DOT DAO owns 100 % at genesis, rate adjustable without
  hard fork.
- The rejected alternative: variable token-to-byte pricing, and Wood's
  four stated objections to it.
- The three governance asks (Hub representation, drip-feed release,
  extraction logic) plus the developer-grant suggestion.

## 5. Where the argument holds
- The multi-use-token distortion is real: locked/staked DOT is not
  available as state collateral, so a DOT-denominated cap has an
  uncertain effective supply.
- Base-layer price-finding genuinely conflicts with JAM's parallel
  accumulate/refine execution model.
- Comparable systems have separated the rent unit from the settlement
  unit or from the free float (Solana rent-exemption, NEAR storage
  staking, Internet Computer cycles, Ethereum's abandoned state-rent
  work).

## 6. Rebuttal — seven lines of attack
- R1 Fixed *supply* is not what enforces the cap; the *rate* does, so the
  soundness claim does not discriminate between DOT and $JAMKB. Only the
  weaker price-signal claim actually distinguishes them.
- R2 NEAR (and Solana) already denominate a capped state deposit in a
  multi-use native token at a network-set per-byte rate.
- R3 P5 conflates a variable *rate* (a cheap stock reprice, O(1) because
  `minbalance` is a dependent term) with a metered *flow* (expensive).
  All four of its objections land only on the flow design. `gratis`
  already covers the developer-grant ask.
- R4 The purity premise is unsatisfiable by any tradable token; $JAMKB
  relocates the competing use from stakers to speculators.
- R5 A constant 1 KB/token *is* a preset price curve — slope zero — so
  "any preset curve is suboptimal" cuts against $JAMKB too.
- R6 Perpetual holding plus no eviction means usable supply is
  monotonically non-increasing. (Real counter-reply: permanence as a
  guarantee; Ethereum rejected state rent on these grounds.)
- R7 Sole DAO issuance makes capacity release political, and Wood's
  dotUSD preference means JAM state demand creates no direct DOT bid.

## 7. Ecosystem response and unresolved questions
- Polkadot Forum threads on distribution, pricing, DOT value capture, and
  perpetual-hold vs. metered-flow.
- Wood's follow-up post and what it concedes or clarifies.
- Governance status: what would actually have to happen for $JAMKB to exist.

## 8. Limitations
- Rate-setting mechanics, initial release schedule, and the Gray Paper's
  final treatment of the footprint token are not yet specified.
- Forum discussion is ecosystem-internal; independent third-party
  economic analysis is thin at this date.
- No implementation exists to measure against.
