# Claims — ai-agent-brief-2026-09-23

- c01: Amazon blocked Meta's Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.com starting the night of 2026-09-21, citing Muse's failure to identify itself as an agent in its HTTP requests and its ability to view a signed-in user's account pages and purchase history.
- c02: Amazon's block of Muse follows a legal precedent it already won in principle: an appeals court dismissed Amazon's suit against Perplexity's Comet browser agent in August 2026 but preserved Amazon's right to block agents that breach its terms of service.
- c03: Security researcher Patrick Wardle disclosed on 2026-09-21 that an undocumented Muse macOS setting let any unprivileged local process redirect the app's dictation traffic to an attacker's server and capture the token authenticating the victim's Muse account, giving remote control over every device signed into that account. Meta shipped a hotfix within about 12-16 hours.
- c04: Between 2026-09-21 and 2026-09-22, the x402 Foundation merged fixes across its Python, TypeScript, and Go reference SDKs closing a route-matching bypass: a literal protected route matched only the framework's escaped request path, while some frameworks (Starlette, Werkzeug) dispatch on the decoded path, so a percent-encoded slash in the URL reached the paid handler without the 402 payment challenge ever firing.
- c05: On 2026-09-22, Identity Digital spun out its Innovation Labs unit as an independent company, Known, to advance DNSid, a DNS/PKI/ledger-based standard meant to give an AI agent a persistent, verifiable identity tied to the organization accountable for it across organizational boundaries; Known has submitted an Internet-Draft to the IETF and is participating in the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation and LF Decentralized Trust groups.
- c06: The three items above share a claim worth stating explicitly: none of the identity/authorization infrastructure now being built (DNSid, x402's own scheme parameters) was in place in time to prevent either the Muse account-hijack bug or the ambiguity Amazon cited over whether Muse was identifying itself as an agent at all.
